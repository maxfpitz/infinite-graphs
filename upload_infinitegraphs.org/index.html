Infinite Graphs

Max Pitz

Graduate Texts in Mathematics, Springer · to appear in early 2027

This first textbook devoted to infinite graphs covers all major themes of modern structural graph theory in the infinite: connectivity, colouring, planarity, spanning trees and tree-decompositions, Euler tours and Hamilton cycles, matchings and minors. Alongside these, it treats purely infinite phenomena such as rays, ends and universal graphs.

About the book

The book grew out of more than twenty years of graduate lectures given at the University of Hamburg. It is written for graduate students and researchers with a background in finite graph theory and an interest in seeing what its infinite counterpart has to offer. No prior knowledge of combinatorics in the infinite is assumed. Part of the text is a hands-on, example-focussed introduction to the techniques it needs, from Zorn’s lemma and transfinite recursion to compactness and closure arguments.

Beginning with the founding results of the subject due to Kőnig, Ramsey and Erdős, the book develops the theory systematically, up to results of the last few years. With a large stock of exercises placed where they become relevant and a focus on open problems that drive research today, it can be used for graduate courses, for self-study, and as an entry point to research.

About the author

Max Pitz received his DPhil from the University of Oxford in 2015 and completed his Habilitation at the University of Hamburg in 2020. He is a tenured lecturer in mathematics there, and has published over 50 research articles in infinite graph theory and analytic topology.
